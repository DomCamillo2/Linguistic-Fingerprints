# Verified literature and model documentation

Last verified: 2026-08-27. Prefer the DOI/official page below over secondary descriptions.

## Core research literature

### Direct predecessor study

**Gude, A., Santos-Rios, R., Bond, F., Flickinger, D., Gómez-Rodríguez, C., & Zamaraeva, O. (2026).** “More Aligned, Less Diverse? Analyzing the Grammar and Lexicon of Two Generations of LLMs.” ACL 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.acl-long.1803) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1803/)

- Compares older base models with newer instruction-tuned systems in English news leads.
- Reports lower syntactic and especially lexical diversity in the newer group.
- Motivates our replication while creating a clear gap: our models are instruction/chat tuned on both sides, matched within family, and tested across five task types.

### Linguistic profiles and model attribution

**Zahid, I., Madusanka, T., Batista-Navarro, R., & Sun, Y. (2024).** “Probing the Uniquely Identifiable Linguistic Patterns of Conversational AI Agents.” Findings of ACL 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.274) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.274/)

- Builds linguistic profiles for five conversational agents using linguistic features, PCA, and classification.
- Demonstrates that model-specific patterns can be measurable, but does not isolate predecessor/successor relations.

**Munir, S., Batool, B., Shafiq, Z., Srinivasan, P., & Zaffar, F. (2021).** “Through the Looking Glass: Learning to Attribute Synthetic Text Generated by Language Models.” EACL 2021. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2021.eacl-main.155) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2021.eacl-main.155/)

- Argues that synthetic texts inherit subtle signals from their source model.
- Its best attribution method is transformer-based; our emphasis is transparent linguistic explanation.

**Uchendu, A., Le, T., Shu, K., & Lee, D. (2020).** “Authorship Attribution for Neural Text Generation.” EMNLP 2020. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2020.emnlp-main.673) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.673/)

- Formulates same-generator, human/machine, and source-generator attribution tasks.
- Establishes model attribution as a research problem distinct from subjective text quality.

### Linguistic diversity

**Guo, Y., Shang, G., & Clavel, C. (2025).** “Benchmarking Linguistic Diversity of Large Language Models.” TACL 13. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1162/tacl.a.47) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.69/)

- Evaluates lexical, syntactic, and semantic diversity in LLM output.
- Supports treating linguistic diversity as an evaluation object in its own right.

### Generalization and leakage risk

**Xu, H., Ren, J., He, P., Zeng, S., Cui, Y., Liu, A., Liu, H., & Tang, J. (2024).** “On the Generalization of Training-based ChatGPT Detection Methods.” Findings of EMNLP 2024. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-emnlp.424) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.424/)

- Studies distribution shifts caused by prompts, lengths, topics, and language tasks.
- Directly motivates prompt-blocked evaluation and length/task audits.

**Xia, Y., Stańczak, K., & Roth, B. (2026).** “Explaining Generalization of AI-Generated Text Detectors Through Linguistic Analysis.” EACL 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.eacl-long.307) · [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-long.307/)

- Evaluates cross-prompt, cross-model, and cross-domain generalization.
- Links generalization performance to shifts in linguistic features such as tense and pronoun use.
